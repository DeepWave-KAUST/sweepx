# 2-Wasserstein (Engquist–Froese / Yang) misfit

## Definition

For two 1-D densities $f, g$ with CDFs $F, G$,

$$
W_2^2(f, g) \;=\; \int_0^1 \bigl(F^{-1}(z) - G^{-1}(z)\bigr)^2\,\mathrm dz.
$$

We sample $z$ uniformly on $(0,1)$ (`n_quantiles` knots, default 256),
linearly interpolate the inverse CDFs from the cumulative sums of the
two trace densities, and Monte-Carlo the integral.  The loss returned is
$\tfrac12 W_2^2$ following the FWI convention of Yang, Engquist, Sun
& Hamfeldt (2018).

Signed traces are first mapped to non-negative densities via the same
`positive` argument as the NIM and W1 losses.

## Key property

For a translated Gaussian density of shift $\Delta t$,

$$
W_2^2 \;=\; (\Delta t)^2,
$$

i.e. $\tfrac12 W_2^2 = \tfrac12 (\Delta t)^2$ which is **quadratic and
convex** in the shift.  This is the cycle-skipping cure proposed by
Engquist & Froese (2014).  Tested in
`test_w2_squared_equals_shift_squared_for_translated_gaussian`.

## Tests

`tests/test_w2.py` checks:

* zero for identical signals across positive transforms,
* analytic identity $\tfrac12 W_2^2 = \tfrac12(\Delta t)^2$ to relative
  5% on translated Gaussians,
* monotone growth with shift,
* **monotone across a range where L2 cycle-skips**,
* gradients flow,
* parameter validation.
